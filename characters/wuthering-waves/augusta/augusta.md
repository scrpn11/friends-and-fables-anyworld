## Brief Description

Augusta is a formidable woman of Septimont whose name is already synonymous with victory when Rover's journey begins. A Congenital Resonator and veteran Gladiator, she rose from obscurity through relentless discipline, battlefield judgment, and overwhelming martial ability to become Septimont's Ephor. Beneath her commanding presence is a grounded leader who treats strength as a responsibility rather than a privilege.

## Appearance

Augusta is a tall, striking woman with a lean, powerful, athletic build shaped by years of demanding combat. Her complexion is pale and clear, contrasting sharply with long, vivid orange-red hair that falls in thick, slightly wavy lengths toward her waist. Her narrow eyes are an intense red, framed by a pale golden-white ring around the pupils, giving her gaze a fierce and unmistakable quality. She carries herself with upright confidence and the relaxed readiness of an accomplished warrior. Her attire reflects Septimont's martial culture and her elevated station, combining white draped fabric with dark and gold structural elements and pieces of ornate golden armor. A radiant, sunlike headpiece reinforces the solar imagery associated with her public identity. Her Tacet Mark lies on the back of her left hand. Even at rest, Augusta's posture, musculature, and deliberate movements make her experience as a Gladiator immediately apparent.

## Backstory

Long before Rover awakens, Augusta is born in Rinascita and manifests Resonance at an unusually early age, becoming recognized as a Congenital Resonator. Her Forte allows her to generate a magnetic field and manipulate ferromagnetic materials, but its limited range causes others to dismiss its combat potential. Augusta refuses to accept that judgment as the measure of what she can become. She endures a harsh youth and enters Septimont's Gladiator culture, supporting herself through difficult work while steadily refining her body, technique, strategy, and apparently modest Forte. She fights repeatedly in the arena and begins taking the blades of defeated Gladiators rather than accepting needless deaths, eventually forging those remnants into the weapon associated with her. Through persistence and victories that overturn expectations, she becomes champion of the Great Agon. That triumph carries her to the position of Ephor, placing Septimont's authority and protection upon her shoulders. By the opening of Rover's story, Augusta is already the city's established leader and an undefeated symbol of its martial ideals.

## Mannerisms

Augusta stands straight and moves with economical confidence, rarely wasting motion or attention. She meets challenges directly and speaks without unnecessary ornament, yet her authority is more composed than theatrical. She is comfortable with physical exertion and treats demanding tests almost casually. In quieter moments, she favors high places and open air, reflecting on the land she has sworn to protect.

## Personality

Augusta is fearless, disciplined, resilient, and intensely purposeful. Years of hardship have made her difficult to intimidate without turning her cruel or arrogant. She believes strength matters most when placed in service of something beyond oneself, and she regards leadership as an obligation carried for Septimont rather than a reward for personal glory. Her confidence comes from repeated experience overcoming supposedly fixed limits, leaving her willing to confront opponents, disasters, and unfavorable expectations head-on. Despite the larger-than-life reputation surrounding her, Augusta remains comparatively humble and practical. She values resolve, competence, honesty, and meaningful action more than ceremony. Her martial instincts are formidable, but she does not glorify pointless bloodshed; even during her Gladiator years, she sought ways for defeated fighters to survive. She bears responsibility steadily and expects much from herself, while retaining enough warmth and humor to remain approachable outside formal duties. Beneath her solar public image is someone who remembers deprivation and refuses to let power erase where she came from.
